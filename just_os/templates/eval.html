Evaluation

The bot answers in three steps, and each table below tests one of them: a topic check decides whether a question is about open science, about FORRT, both, or neither (then it refuses); a search finds passages in the paper library (or the bot reads forrt.org pages); an answer model writes the answer and cites its sources as [n]. Each section says what was tested, on which questions, what the columns mean and how the best one is chosen. One row per model or setting: its newest run; every run is in the run history. Files: eval/results/; background: docs/evaluation.md.

Answer models

What was tested

Every model answered every question twice, without a topic check: once from the papers (the same 7 passages for every model, found once by the search and re-ranker, so only the model differs) and once from the forrt.org pages. Two LLM judges then read each answer next to its sources: the citation judge (eval/judge.py, gpt-oss-20b) and, for the questions with a rubric, the rubric judge (eval/rubric_judge.py, gemini-3.8-flash).

How the score is calculated

Score = (2 × Judge + Rubric coverage) / 3

Both are 0 to 1. Citation support counts double: a cited sentence its source does not support misleads more than a missing point, and the judge covers about 160 answers per model where the rubric covers 25. The default answer model is the best score among runs that answered every question without errors.

Topic check

What was tested

The topic check alone (eval/classify.py): for each question it picks one of four routes: open science (answer from the papers), FORRT (answer from forrt.org), both, or neither (refuse). The pick is compared with the question's gold route. Strategies: {% for k, v in routers.items() if k %}{{ v }}{% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %}.

How the default is chosen

The most accurate strategy in the newest run that compared several on the same questions; on a tie, the cheaper one (fewer model calls), then the faster one. Rows from older runs may use other questions: compare rows with the same n.

Topic check × answer model

What was tested

The whole bot for every pair of topic check and answer model, on the same questions (eval/pairs.py). The topic check only decides where a question goes, so for each question the pair's answer is the model's answer from the papers (open science or both), from the forrt.org pages (FORRT), or a refusal (neither): the runs of the two sections above, combined.

How the best pair is chosen

The share of questions handled correctly: answered when it should answer, refused when it should refuse (an error counts as wrong). Ties go to the higher judge score. Cells are greener the higher this is.

What was tested

People and judges

What was tested

People on /review and LLM judges score the same stored answers on the same criteria and scale (config/criteria.json, #54): the answer store is data/answers/, the scores data/scores/. Gold metrics (the expected decision, hit@7) count as scorers too.

How to read it

A judge can stand in for people on a criterion when it ranks answers as they do (Spearman near 1) and its mean is close to theirs. Two reviewers who agree little on a criterion (weighted kappa near 0) mean its definition needs sharpening before it can check a judge. Few answers make every number rough.

Agreement
For the decision (answer or refuse), the share of answers where people chose what the scorer did. Otherwise Spearman's rank correlation between people's mean score per answer and the scorer's: 1 when both put the answers in the same order, 0 when the orders are unrelated.
