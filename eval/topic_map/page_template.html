3 · Pre-LLM gate

Can we skip the LLM when we have nothing?

Gate score = the closest chunk among the papers and the FORRT pages to the question, computed with the same bge-m3 embedding the bot already runs (local, or $0.01 per million tokens on OpenRouter). Below, the __QUESTIONS__ single-turn questions of eval/questions.jsonl, labelled by the team as "should answer" or "should refuse". Move the threshold.

–answerable questions kept

Clear off-topic: free refusal

Everyday trivia (microwaves, rainbows, the deepest ocean) scores below 0.55, and no answerable question does. A 0.55 cut-off refuses them before any LLM call. The margin is small: "How do birds fly?" scores 0.547, the lowest answerable question 0.563.

Near-topic: still needs the classifier

Career advice, grant writing, SPSS help and "which journal" score 0.56–0.73, the same range as real open-science questions. Embedding similarity cannot tell these apart, so the LLM classifier stays for the middle band. The new papers, the large handbooks above all, raised off-topic scores by up to 0.06.

Low score inside scope means a gap

The cluster papers fixed named replication effects (power posing: 0 → 5 close papers, ego depletion: 4 → 13) and citizen science (3 → 11). Still thin: persistent identifiers ("Is there any free PID?": 0 papers), ORCID (1), Gold OA (2) and ghost authorship (2).

4 · Data problems found

Wrong files and wrong titles

Comparing each paper's title with the meaning of its own chunks found two problems. In six JUST-OS papers the text has nothing to do with the title: the DOI in the FORRT sheet points to another work and Unpaywall downloaded that work. In some FORRT cluster papers the text is right but the title is wrong, because the PDF converter took a page header, a licence line or a mismatched DOI as the title. The bot cites both under the wrong title.

16% of chunks are reference lists or near-empty

22,860 chunks are mostly citations, author lists or markup: 22% of the JUST-OS chunks but only 8% of the cluster chunks, because GROBID drops the reference list and marker does not. 11,916 JUST-OS chunks still carry marker's <span id="page-…"> anchors. They form 33 junk topics on the map and take retrieval slots from real text.

61 topics come mostly from one paper

Three long documents dominate the biggest of these: Haklay 2018 on citizen science (1,159 of its 2,096 chunks in one topic), The SAGE Handbook of Qualitative Research (1,060) and Reproducible Research with R and RStudio (921). A sub-cluster that looks covered may rest on one book, and the books also pull off-topic questions closer.

5 · Next steps

What to do with this

  1. Fix the six wrong files. Correct the DOIs in the FORRT sheet and drop their chunks from the index (1,741 chunks).
  2. Fix the cluster-paper titles. The seven wrong titles above, plus the 29 papers whose title is still the file name (for example C09 SC08 Incl Henrich 2010). Users see these titles in the source list.
  3. Use the gap list for content. The free similarity check now exists as the banded topic check (ROUTER=banded, docs/evaluation.md 3.11). An in-scope question with a low score points to missing papers, not to a question to refuse.
  4. Drop reference-list chunks from the JUST-OS papers: filter them at query time, or convert those papers with GROBID when the index is next rebuilt.
  5. Check the seven remaining blind spots by hand. Equity, Public–Private Partnerships, Religion and culture, Sexuality, Feminist Thought, Inclusion and Rights retention. Equity and Inclusion each have 20 PDFs in their folders, yet few papers sit close to the name. Either the PDFs cover the topic in other words (a short one-word name is a weak probe), or the material is thin. Then add sources for PIDs/ORCID, Gold OA and ghost authorship.
